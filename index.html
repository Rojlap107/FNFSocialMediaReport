Executive Summary

This report covers FNF India's social media performance across Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn from April 6 to May 3, 2026 — coinciding with the FNF India Delegation's four-city Europe Tour (Brussels, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Stuttgart) and the Dalai Lama's 90th Birthday celebrations.

Instagram and LinkedIn are the primary growth drivers. Facebook remains our most-followed platform (10,075 followers) but shows audience saturation and limited engagement depth. LinkedIn is delivering the highest-quality engagement from professionals in India, Germany, and Europe — directly aligned with FNF India's programmatic priorities.

Key highlight: Instagram views surged to 53.8K (+46.2%) and LinkedIn impressions spiked on Apr 27 (515) and Apr 29 (777), driven by Europe tour content.

Instagram is the Growth Engine
With 53.8K views (+46.2%), Instagram is clearly the highest-reach platform. The 81.4% of views from non-followers signals strong content discoverability — content is breaking through to new audiences beyond just the existing community.
Europe Tour Drove All Platforms
The Brussels, Hamburg, and Frankfurt delegation events created clear spikes across all three platforms — most visible in LinkedIn (Apr 24–29) and Instagram (Apr 20–27). Policy-focused, event-driven content consistently outperformed all other post types.
Facebook Growth Has Plateaued
With 10,075 followers and +0.0% growth, Facebook is stagnant. While engagement improved (+26.8%), 3-second video views fell -58% and 1-minute views fell -88%, indicating video content is not being consumed deeply on this platform.
LinkedIn Quality Engagement is High
The Brussels delegation post achieved a 65.6% engagement rate. The Hamburg Trade Conference post saw 547 clicks from 1,188 impressions (46% CTR). LinkedIn is clearly the most effective channel for reaching policymakers and professionals.

Video Content Not Landing on Facebook
Despite views being up 21%, video metrics crashed: 3-second views fell 58%, 1-minute views down 88%, and total watch time dropped 81%. Videos are being scrolled past — either the hook isn't strong enough or Facebook's algorithm is limiting Reel distribution to this audience.
Carousels Are the Winning Format
Multi-photo content claims 58.4% of all views — nearly 3x the next-best format (Photo at 13.4%). This confirms that the carousel format is FNF India's most effective content type on Facebook. Doubling down here is a no-brainer.
Zero Comments — A Conversation Gap
0 comments in the entire reporting period is a critical signal. The audience is not finding content compelling enough to respond to. Comments signal algorithmic trust; their absence limits organic reach. Strategy should experiment with direct questions and calls to action.

Views Are Surging — Algorithm Favouring Content
A 46.2% surge in views to 53.8K is exceptional. With 81.4% of views coming from non-followers, Instagram's algorithm is actively surfacing FNF India content to new users — a strong signal that content quality and topic relevance is working. The Europe tour content drove massive discovery.
High Views but Declining Interactions — Conversion Gap
Despite 53.8K views, interactions fell 34.9% to ~1K. Content is being seen but not actively engaged with (liked, commented, saved). Stronger CTAs, interactive stories (polls, questions), and more relatable captions could help close this gap.
Follower Growth Stalled — Follows = Unfollows
62 new follows and 62 unfollows means net zero growth. Given the high non-follower reach, the conversion rate from viewer to follower is very low. With strong discovery numbers, converting those viewers into followers should be the #1 priority next cycle.

LinkedIn is Punching Above Its Weight
With engagement rates between 21%–65% on top posts, FNF India's LinkedIn is performing extraordinarily well. The Hamburg post's 46% CTR (547 clicks from 1,188 impressions) is exceptional even by B2B LinkedIn standards. The policy-focused professional audience is highly engaged with FNF India's European partnership work.
Long-form Articles Outperform Videos
Every top-performing LinkedIn post was a detailed article/text post — not a video or short-form. The Brussels (65.6% eng.) and Frankfurt event invite (69.8% eng.) posts were text-heavy narratives. LinkedIn's professional audience responds to depth and substance.
Zero Comments Across All Posts
Similar to Facebook, LinkedIn saw zero comments across the entire reporting period. High click rates suggest people are consuming content externally (clicking links), but the conversation isn't happening on LinkedIn itself. Asking explicit questions and tagging relevant European partners could stimulate discussion.

Facebook Audience Doesn't Match FNF India's Core Target
Facebook's top cities are Dhaka (25.8%), Kathmandu (13.5%), and Delhi (11.7%) — with Pakistan at 26.7% of total audience. While South Asian reach is valuable for issue awareness, FNF India's programmatic focus on India–Europe policy and liberal governance doesn't directly serve audiences in Bangladesh and Pakistan.
Instagram's Germany Audience is a Strategic Asset
Germany appearing as the 3rd-largest country on Instagram (8.4%) is highly valuable. Given FNF's German identity and FNF India's EU–India work, this audience directly aligns with programmatic goals. Berlin (1.5%) is among the top cities. This audience should be nurtured with EU–India partnership content.
25-34 Core Audience Consistent Across Platforms
Both Facebook (50.8%) and Instagram (~55%) show the 25–34 age group as dominant. This is the educated young professional segment — ideal for FNF India's liberal democracy, policy, and education messaging. Content designed for early-career professionals should continue to perform well.

Strategic Insights & Recommendations

Analysis-based recommendations for the next reporting cycle  ·  May–June 2026

Overall Assessment

FNF India's social media is on an upward trajectory — but conversion needs attention.

Performance in April–May 2026 is driven primarily by high-value event content from the Europe Delegation Tour. The portfolio now has a clear two-tier structure: Instagram and LinkedIn are growth platforms where reach and engagement quality are strong; Facebook is a legacy platform with a large but stagnant follower base and declining video metrics.

The most important takeaway: event-driven, substantive, long-form content consistently outperforms all other content types across all three platforms. The Brussels delegation post (LinkedIn: 65.6% eng. rate), Hamburg Trade Conference post (547 clicks), and "Politics & Pretzels" Instagram post (12.2K views) are all rooted in real, high-stakes programmatic work — not manufactured social media content.

The challenge going forward is maintaining content quality consistency outside of major event windows, and converting high view numbers into active community engagement (comments, saves, shares).

01 —
Double Down on Carousels (FB) and Stories (IG)
Facebook data confirms multi-photo carousels account for 58.4% of all views. Instagram published 58 stories (+184% vs prior period) — this volume is working. Continue using stories for real-time event updates and carousels for structured policy explainers. Reels on Facebook should be deprioritized given the steep drop in video watch metrics.
02 —
Invest in LinkedIn Long-form Content
LinkedIn's best performers were all long, substantive articles with narrative depth — not short captions or reposts. Allocate time to write proper 200–400 word LinkedIn posts for every major FNF India event or publication. The engagement rate payoff is enormous (65%+). Tag European partner organizations and speakers to extend reach organically.
03 —
Add Calls-to-Action to Drive Comments
Zero comments across Facebook and LinkedIn is a strategic gap. End every post with a direct question ("What's your take on the EU–India FTA?", "How should India's youth engage globally?"). Comments signal algorithmic quality and extend organic reach. Even 5–10 comments per post would meaningfully improve distribution.
04 —
Convert Instagram Viewers into Followers
With 81.4% of Instagram views coming from non-followers and net zero follower growth, there's a significant conversion failure. Use Instagram Stories to prompt "Follow for more India–Europe updates," create save-worthy content (infographics, key data), and ensure each post bio link and call-to-action is clear and updated.
05 —
Create Content Bridges Between Platforms
The same event (e.g., Hamburg Trade Conference) should have platform-specific content: a detailed LinkedIn article, a visual carousel on Facebook, and a real-time Story series on Instagram. Currently posts appear to be cross-posted without platform optimization. Tailoring tone and format per platform will significantly improve performance across all three.
06 —
Leverage Germany Audience on Instagram
Germany is FNF India's 3rd-largest Instagram audience at 8.4% — strategically valuable. Content on EU–India relations, FNF Germany collaborations, and Indian diaspora in Germany should be explicitly targeted at this segment. Consider bilingual captions (English + German) for key posts, and tag FNF Germany (@fnf_de) to amplify reach into the German liberal network.

Maintain Event-led Content Rhythm
The single biggest driver of performance this period was real events with real stakeholders producing real content — not manufactured social media posts. The Europe Tour gave the team 3+ weeks of high-value, authentic content. Planning for the next major content window (upcoming programmes, publications, partnerships) should begin now.
Set Up a Content Calendar for Between Events
Between major events, engagement dropped sharply across all platforms. A 4-week content calendar with planned themes (FNF India research, partner spotlights, policy explainers, youth voices) will ensure consistent posting and audience retention. Even 3 posts per week per platform (scheduled) would prevent the post-event dip.
Consider Facebook Audience Recalibration
With 26.7% Pakistan and 16.7% Bangladesh audiences on Facebook — and content focused primarily on India–Europe policy — there may be a values-alignment gap worth addressing. If budget allows, running targeted paid posts to Indian policy and professional audiences could begin recalibrating the follower base toward the strategic target audience.
